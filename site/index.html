Kiehl Laboratory

The Kiehl Lab uses brain imaging to understand psychopathy and related conditions: psychotic disorders, traumatic brain injury, substance use and paraphilias. Led by principal investigator Kent A. Kiehl, PhD, at the nonprofit Mind Research Network and the University of New Mexico, we take a mobile MRI scanner into prisons and treatment facilities, and work with judges, lawyers and the public on what neuroscience can and cannot tell the legal system.

How we work

A scanner that goes to the people being studied

The people who most need to be studied can rarely travel to a university scanner. Kent Kiehl designed the Mind Mobile MRI System so the lab can bring one to them, and the resulting collection of brain scans, interviews and follow-up records is the largest forensic neuroscience repository in the world.

About the lab

In Albuquerque since 2007

Principal investigator Kent Kiehl trained at the University of California, Davis, and at the University of British Columbia with Robert Hare and Peter Liddle, where he published the first fMRI study of brain dysfunction in criminal psychopaths. He led the Clinical Cognitive Neuroscience Laboratory at the Institute of Living and Yale University from 2001 to 2007, when the lab moved to the Mind Research Network and the University of New Mexico.
